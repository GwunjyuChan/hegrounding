The existence of the following letters requires some 
explanation. 

On looking over the papers of my father, at his 
decease, I found all my letters preserved. 

Some time since it occurred to me to examine them, 
with a view to their destruction. I became, however, 
interested in the journal, and set about transcribing it, 
which was accomplished in the leisure afforded by 
autumnal visits to relatives. 

Friends to whom I showed the manuscripts expressed 
gratification in the perusal. Talking with a valued 
acquaintance, familiar with literary pursuits, of what I 
had been doing, he desired to see the letters, and 
returned them with warm suggestions for their publi- 
cation. 

From similar observations, the result is that the 
letters are now committed to the press with some diffi- 
dence, but not without a humble trust that it may 
please God to accompany them with His blessing. 

JOHN SPEELING.

Chatham liad been my station for a short time, when,

much in each other's society. This was mutually 
advantageous, and our intimacy tended both to our 
profit and enjoyment. Dining at the mess, our other 
meals were in common, taJdng them alternately at each 
other's rooms. 

In forwarding our orders from Pall Mall, Colonel 
Handfield had kindly enclosed a private note, directing 
us to come to London, to provide the necessary equip-
